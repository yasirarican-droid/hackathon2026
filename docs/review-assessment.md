# Jury review: decisions and remaining limits

The theme is now a first-class entry point on home and each console: Where is it now?, with condition, reason for staying, ending, dated original orbital evidence and visible NSSDCA record. Uncertainty is preserved for Eagle's upper stage and Sojourner's final movements. The original Eagle landing clip is available from the phone story; modern narration remains distinct.

Pressure exploration now includes seasonal CO2 freezing and return to the atmosphere, alongside the original Viking measurements. The general climate mechanism is not claimed to isolate the cause of every point in a 30-sol series. Sources and a qualitative two-state activity support the explanation.

We keep three missions rather than enlarging the room count. The intended depth is a complete evidence-to-question-to-explanation loop: inspect hardware and original imagery, compare measured or documented information, answer questions, locate the abandoned equipment and reflect on its ending. A larger catalog is a different product scope; no competitor implementation has been copied or runtime-audited.

Readable headings replace ambiguous brush lettering, while CRTs use a digital font. Full guide bubbles open only on request. Small invitation bubbles remain. Replay story consolidates entry replay, arrival and CRT introduction. The single complete preloader and landscape-only rule remain owner decisions. Live timings are measurements from one automated Chrome run, not promises for every judge device. Actual latest results are in docs/qa/loading-after.json and loading-cached.json.

The independent-project disclaimer is visible on home and Info. It does not imply legal clearance for the domain. Team roles and AI contribution are explicit: humans selected and reviewed the missions, evidence, art direction, interactions and supplied recordings; tools assisted production. No learning-effectiveness claim or fabricated child study has been added. The existing observation protocol still needs real participants.

## Suggested 40-second presentation opening

“We chose three explorers and followed their stories in depth: Eagle, Viking 1 and Sojourner. Our question is what happens after a mission goes quiet. Children investigate original NASA photographs, NSSDCA records and measured weather data, then explain what they found. Each room shows where the hardware remains, why it was left and what is still uncertain. Our team directed and reviewed the research, design and interactions; AI tools assisted implementation, illustrations and supplied narration. The final certificate recognizes completing the exploration, not passing a NASA test.”

## Remaining limits

Real testing with children, platform submission requirements and final competition-rule review remain human tasks. Consolidated illustration credits should not be described as a verified replacement for the competition watermark requirement. The existing demo video may show an earlier build and should be re-recorded for final submission. No additional mission rooms or speculative radio messages were added.
